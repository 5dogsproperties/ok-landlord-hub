Pet Fee vs Pet Deposit vs Pet Rent: How Oklahoma Landlords Should Label Them

A tenant wants to bring a dog. Do you charge a fee, a deposit, pet rent, or some mix? The name you put on each charge decides which rules follow it. Here's how to label each one clearly in an Oklahoma lease. The full deposit rules, with cites, are on the RentStatute security deposit page.

1. Quick answer

A refundable pet deposit is a damage or security deposit, so 41 O.S. §115 applies: it goes in an Oklahoma escrow account, and deductions must be itemized in writing at move-out. We found no Oklahoma statute that specifically addresses non-refundable pet fees, so if you charge one, label it as non-refundable in the lease. Pet rent is a recurring charge, and when it's a required payment under the lease it is generally treated as rent. Service and assistance animals are not pets (see the caution below).

3. Why the label matters

Section 115 covers "any damage or security deposit" a landlord requires. If money is called a deposit, or you hold it as security against damage, the escrow, itemized-statement, and 45-day return rules attach. Mixed signals cause trouble: a "non-refundable pet deposit," or a "fee" you later deduct damage from like a deposit.

Courts can look at what a charge actually does, not just its name. We found no Oklahoma case recharacterizing a pet fee, but that doesn't mean a label alone will protect you. Make the name, the lease wording, and how you handle the money all match. Mechanics are on the RentStatute security deposit page.

4. Pet deposit

  • Treat it like any other security deposit: escrow it in Oklahoma with a federally insured financial institution (§115(A)).
  • At move-out, apply it to accrued rent and damages in an itemized written statement, and return any balance within 45 days after the tenancy ends, the tenant gives up possession, and the tenant makes a written demand (§115(B)).
  • Say in the lease what it covers, such as pet damage beyond normal wear and tear.
  • Practice: take dated move-in and move-out photos of floors, doors, trim, and yard.

5. Pet fee

We found no Oklahoma statute that specifically addresses non-refundable pet fees. The prohibited lease terms in 41 O.S. §113 don't mention them. Finding no statute is not a green light, so keep the wording plain:

  • Call it a "pet fee" and say it is non-refundable.
  • Say what it's for, such as cleaning, administration, or wear related to having a pet.
  • Don't call it a deposit or describe it as security for damage.

Practice: the Oklahoma Real Estate Commission's 2026 Pet Addendum form describes its pet fee as not refunded and not escrowed or set aside to pay for pet damages. That's a useful wording model, not a legal requirement.

6. Pet rent

Pet rent is a recurring charge on top of base rent. Oklahoma doesn't have a separate pet rent statute. Under 41 O.S. §102(11), "rent" means all payments, except deposits and damages, to be made to the landlord under the rental agreement. So a recurring pet charge that is a required payment under the lease is generally treated as rent, including for the delinquent-rent notice in §131. State the amount, whether it's per pet or per household, and the due date in the lease.

7. Combining charges and adding a pet mid-lease (Practice)

We found no Oklahoma statute limiting how pet charges are combined. If you use more than one, list each separately with its own label and refund terms. When a tenant asks to add a pet mid-lease, use a written pet addendum signed by both sides before the animal moves in.

8. How much to charge

We don't publish figures. Set amounts in your lease based on your property and local market, and check with counsel. Section 115 sets no dollar cap on deposits, and we found no other Title 41 deposit-cap statute in this review.

9. Pet addendum clause checklist (Practice)

  • Label each charge: pet deposit, pet fee, or pet rent.
  • Refundable: yes or no, for each charge.
  • Per pet or per household.
  • What the deposit covers, and that damage beyond it is still owed.
  • Pet rent amount and due date.
  • Written approval required before any animal arrives; describe each approved pet.
  • Vaccination and local registration, if your city requires it.
  • Signatures and date from landlord and every tenant.
CAUTION: Service and assistance animals aren't pets. Under fair housing law, they are generally treated differently from pets. Don't apply pet fees, deposits, or pet rent to an accommodation request without checking current HUD guidance and an Oklahoma attorney. See fair housing basics.

FAQ

Is a pet deposit refundable in Oklahoma?

A refundable pet deposit is a damage or security deposit, so 41 O.S. §115 applies: escrow in Oklahoma, itemized written deductions, and return of any balance within 45 days after the tenancy ends, the tenant gives up possession, and the tenant makes a written demand. See the RentStatute security deposit page.

Can I charge a non-refundable pet fee in Oklahoma?

We found no Oklahoma statute that specifically addresses non-refundable pet fees. That is not the same as a statute allowing them. If you charge one, label it clearly as non-refundable in the lease, say what it covers, and don't hold it as security. Ask an Oklahoma attorney about your wording.

Is there a cap on pet deposits in Oklahoma?

Section 115 sets no dollar cap, and we found no other Title 41 deposit-cap statute in this review. Laws change; check the current statute and local rules.

Does a pet deposit have to go in escrow?

Yes, if it is a damage or security deposit. 41 O.S. §115(A) says any damage or security deposit must be kept in an escrow account in Oklahoma with a federally insured financial institution.

Can I charge pet rent and a pet deposit?

We found no Oklahoma statute that limits combining pet charges. Each one should be labeled separately in the lease, and the deposit part still follows §115.

Do pet charges apply to service or assistance animals?

Don't assume so. See the caution box above, check current HUD guidance, and talk to an Oklahoma attorney before handling an accommodation request.
