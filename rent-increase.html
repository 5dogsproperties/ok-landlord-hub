How to raise rent in Oklahoma (and how much notice to give)

What to check before a renewal or a month-to-month increase, how to give notice, and how to avoid an increase that looks like retaliation.

Informational only — not legal advice. Statute summaries are plain English, not the statute text; read the section and ask counsel before relying on it. Items marked Practice are operating habits, not law. John has owned 300+ units since 2014.

Legal points last verified: October 19, 2026, against 41 O.S. on OSCN/Justia. Statutes change; read the section before relying on it.

1. The short answer

"The lease is up in March and I want to raise the rent." Here's what to check first.

What we can cite

  • A fixed-term lease sets the rent for the term. Raise it at renewal, or mid-term only if the lease allows it. (Contract terms; check your lease and ask counsel.)
  • A month-to-month tenancy can be ended with 30 days' written notice, running from the date of service. 41 O.S. §111 (Justia 2025 · OSCN), (A)
  • A fixed-term lease ends on its end date without notice, unless the lease says otherwise. 41 O.S. §111 (Justia 2025 · OSCN), (C)

2. Is there a cap or rent control?

What this page covers

  • Oklahoma's Landlord-Tenant Act sets no cap on rent increases and no notice rule specific to rent increases.
  • Oklahoma law bars cities and towns from enacting, keeping or enforcing any ordinance that regulates the amount of rent for privately owned residential or commercial rental property. Exceptions cover city-owned property, rent agreements for subsidized units, and units assisted with federal CDBG funds. 11 O.S. §14-101.1 (Justia 2025)

3. Fixed-term lease vs. month-to-month

Fixed-term lease

Practice: Send the renewal offer, with the new rent, well before the term ends so the tenant has time to decide.

  • Rent is set by the lease for the term
  • Mid-term increases depend on the lease wording; ask counsel if the lease is silent
  • At the end of the term the lease ends on its end date unless the lease says otherwise. 41 O.S. §111 (Justia 2025 · OSCN), (C)

Month-to-month

Practice: Because either side can end a month-to-month tenancy on 30 days' written notice (41 O.S. §111 (Justia 2025 · OSCN), (A)), give at least 30 days' written notice of a new rent, starting on a rent due date. The Act has no separate increase-notice rule; confirm with counsel.

  • Either side can end the tenancy with 30 days' written notice. 41 O.S. §111 (Justia 2025 · OSCN), (A)
  • If you hold over a tenant with your consent after a lease ends, the tenancy becomes month-to-month, unless you agree otherwise. 41 O.S. §111 (Justia 2025 · OSCN), (D)

4. How to give notice of an increase

Practice

Serve increase notices the way the Act requires for termination notices (41 O.S. §111 (Justia 2025 · OSCN), (E)): personally; if the tenant can't be located, to a family member over 12 who lives there; if neither works, post it conspicuously on the unit and mail a copy by certified mail or the USPS Firm Mailing Book. The Act requires this for termination notices, not increases. Hand notices only to an adult 18+ as a stricter habit.

  • Put it in writing, dated and signed
  • State the current rent, the new rent and the date it starts
  • Deliver it the same way you serve other notices, and keep proof

5. Retaliation and discrimination

Practice

Oklahoma's Landlord-Tenant Act has no general anti-retaliation section. Federal fair-housing law still bars coercion, intimidation, threats or interference against anyone exercising fair-housing rights (42 U.S.C. §3617 (Cornell LII)), and subsidized or federally backed units may have their own rules. Ask counsel.

  • Don't raise rent right after a tenant makes a repair request or complaint; space it from any open repair ticket and document a business reason
  • Apply the same increase policy to every tenant in similar units
  • Federally backed or subsidized units and voucher tenants may have separate federal rules; check them before any increase
  • Fair-housing law applies to rent increases like any other term: never base an increase on a protected class

6. Renewal checklist and a sample increase letter

Renewal checklist

  • Pull the lease end date and current rent
  • Check open repair tickets and tenant payment history
  • Decide the new rent and the start date
  • Send the written offer or notice, and log how and when it was delivered
  • Get the renewal signed, or calendar the move-out

Sample increase letter

Sample wording, for attorney review before use: "Your monthly rent at [address] will change from $[current] to $[new] starting [date]. Please confirm by [date]. Questions: [phone]."

  • Tenant name, unit address, date
  • Current monthly rent and new monthly rent
  • Date the new rent starts
  • How to accept, and the deadline
  • Your name, phone and signature

FAQ

How much can a landlord raise rent in Oklahoma?

Oklahoma sets no cap: the Landlord-Tenant Act has no limit on increases, and Oklahoma cities can't cap private rents (11 O.S. §14-101.1); federal program rules may still apply. Check your lease and ask counsel.

Can I raise rent during a lease?

A fixed-term lease sets the rent for the term. A mid-term increase depends on what the lease says; ask counsel if it's silent.

How much notice do I give a month-to-month tenant?

Oklahoma requires 30 days' written notice to end a month-to-month tenancy (41 O.S. §111). The Act doesn't set a separate rule for increases, so a common practice is at least 30 days' written notice, starting on a rent due date. Confirm with counsel.

Can I raise rent after a tenant complains about repairs?

Oklahoma's Landlord-Tenant Act has no general anti-retaliation section, but federal fair-housing law bars coercion, intimidation, threats or interference against anyone exercising fair-housing rights (42 U.S.C. §3617). Ask counsel. As a practice, keep increases separate from open repair tickets and document the business reason.

Do I need to put the increase in writing?

Put it in writing, dated, with the old rent, new rent and start date, and keep proof of delivery.
