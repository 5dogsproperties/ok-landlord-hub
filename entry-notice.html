Landlord entry in Oklahoma: notice, timing and refused access

When you can enter, how much notice to give, what counts as an emergency, and what to do when a tenant won't let you in, with the Landlord-Tenant Act cited where it applies.

Informational only — not legal advice. Statute summaries are plain English, not the statute text; read the section and ask counsel before relying on it. Items marked Practice are operating habits, not law. John has owned 300+ units since 2014.

Legal points last verified: October 19, 2026, against 41 O.S. on OSCN/Justia. Statutes change; read the section before relying on it.

1. The short answer

"I need to get into the unit Thursday to look at the water heater." Here's what Oklahoma law requires before you go.

What the Act says

  • Outside an emergency, give the tenant at least 1 day's notice of your intent to enter, and enter only at reasonable times. 41 O.S. §128 (Justia 2025 · OSCN)
  • In an emergency you may enter without the tenant's consent. 41 O.S. §128 (Justia 2025 · OSCN)
  • The tenant can't unreasonably refuse entry to inspect, make repairs, supply agreed services, or show the unit. 41 O.S. §128 (Justia 2025 · OSCN)

2. Reasons you may enter

Purposes named in §128

Source: 41 O.S. §128 (Justia 2025 · OSCN).

  • Inspect the unit
  • Make necessary or agreed repairs, decorations, alterations or improvements
  • Supply necessary or agreed services
  • Show the unit to prospective or actual buyers, mortgage lenders, tenants, workers or contractors

Practice

  • Write the reason on every notice
  • Bundle visits: one entry for the inspection, the filter change and the smoke-alarm test
  • Keep an entry log: date, time, who entered, why

3. How much notice, and how to give it

The rule

  • At least 1 day's notice, unless notice is impracticable or it's an emergency. 41 O.S. §128 (Justia 2025 · OSCN)
  • Enter only at reasonable times. The Act doesn't define "reasonable times"; set your window in the lease.

Practice

  • Give notice in writing (email, portal, or a posted notice) so you have a record
  • State the date, a time window and the reason
  • Text messages: whether a text counts as notice isn't settled by the Act. Put the notice methods you'll use in the lease, and keep a copy
  • Bring a vendor? Name the company on the notice

4. Emergencies: when notice isn't required

What the Act says

  • You may enter without consent in an emergency. 41 O.S. §128 (Justia 2025 · OSCN)

Practice

  • Treat as emergencies: fire, gas smell, active water leak or flooding, sparking or burning smell from wiring
  • After an emergency entry, leave a written note and send a follow-up message the same day explaining what you did
  • Photograph the problem and the fix

5. What if the tenant refuses access?

Your options under the Act

  • If the tenant refuses lawful access, you may get a court order (injunction) to compel access, or terminate the lease. 41 O.S. §128 (Justia 2025 · OSCN), (E)
  • Beyond these rules, you have no other right of access during a tenancy unless the tenant has abandoned or surrendered the unit or a court orders it. 41 O.S. §128 (Justia 2025 · OSCN), (D)
  • Belongings left after abandonment, surrender or eviction have their own rules; talk to counsel. 41 O.S. §130 (Justia 2025)

Practice

  • Offer two or three alternative times in writing before escalating
  • Document each refusal with the date and the tenant's words
  • Talk to counsel before terminating over refused access
  • Never change the locks or remove belongings: a lockout is wrongful exclusion, and the tenant can recover possession or end the lease and recover up to twice the monthly rent or twice actual damages, whichever is greater. 41 O.S. §123 (Justia 2025 · OSCN)

6. Abusing the right of entry

What the Act says

  • You may not abuse the right of access or use it to harass the tenant. 41 O.S. §128 (Justia 2025 · OSCN)
  • If you enter unlawfully, enter lawfully but in an unreasonable manner, or harass the tenant with repeated unreasonable demands for entry, the tenant may get an injunction or, with written notice, end the lease, and in either case recover actual damages. 41 O.S. §124 (Justia 2025)

Practice

  • Don't enter repeatedly without a real reason
  • Don't drop by without notice for non-emergencies
  • Knock and announce yourself before using a key, even with notice

7. Checklist: an entry notice that holds up

Sample entry notice

Sample wording, for attorney review before use: "Under 41 O.S. §128, we will enter [address] on [date] between [time] and [time] to [reason]. [Name/company] will be present. Questions: [phone]."

  • Tenant name and unit address
  • Date of the notice
  • Date and time window of entry (at least 1 day after the notice)
  • Reason for entry
  • Who will enter (you, a named vendor)
  • Your name and phone number

Records to keep

  • Copy of every notice and how it was delivered
  • Entry log
  • Photos from the visit
  • Any refusal, in writing

FAQ

Can a landlord enter without permission in Oklahoma?

In an emergency, yes. Otherwise you must give at least 1 day's notice and enter at reasonable times, and the tenant can't unreasonably refuse entry for inspections, repairs, agreed services or showings (41 O.S. §128).

How much notice does an Oklahoma landlord have to give before entering?

At least 1 day, unless notice is impracticable or it's an emergency (41 O.S. §128).

Does a text message count as notice?

The Act doesn't say. Put the notice methods you'll use in the lease, and keep a copy of every notice.

Can I show the unit to prospective tenants before the lease ends?

Yes. Showing the unit to prospective tenants is one of the purposes listed in 41 O.S. §128. Give at least 1 day's notice and come at reasonable times.

What should I do if my tenant keeps refusing entry?

Document each refusal and offer other times in writing. If the tenant refuses lawful access, the Act lets you seek an injunction or terminate the lease (41 O.S. §128). Talk to counsel first, and never change the locks.

Can I enter after a tenant abandons the unit?

This page doesn't cover abandonment; talk to counsel. Abandoned belongings have their own notice rules under 41 O.S. §130.
