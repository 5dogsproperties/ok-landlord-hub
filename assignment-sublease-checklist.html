Your Tenant Wants to Hand Off the Lease — 7 Steps for Oklahoma Landlords

A student, job transfer, or roommate change shows up with a friend who “will take over the lease.” Here is a short practical list. Statute-cited answers — what §10 says, assignment vs sublease definitions, the §11 remedy, and original-tenant liability — are on the RentStatute lease assignment vs sublease page.

1. Get the request in writing (Practice)

  • Current tenant name, unit, and lease end date.
  • Proposed person's full name and contact info.
  • Whether they want the rest of the term or a shorter stay.
  • Proposed move dates and who will pay rent going forward.

Oklahoma's written-assent rule for many short-term and at-will tenancies is explained on RentStatute under what 41 O.S. §10 actually says.

2. Decide the paperwork path (Practice)

  • Leaving for good → often a new lease with the incoming person is cleaner than an assignment. See when a new lease is cleaner.
  • Temporary shorter handoff → document carefully. Call it a “sublease” only with the common-law definitions on RentStatute — §10 never uses that word. See assignment vs sublease.
  • Roommate added → use a lease addendum naming the new occupant; still screen and get written assent if the lease interest is being transferred in part.

3. Screen the incoming person like any applicant (Practice)

Use the same written criteria you use for every applicant. Stay consistent with fair housing rules. See the Hub page on fair housing and families with kids and RentStatute's screening notes.

4. Put written assent on paper (Practice)

If you approve, sign a dated assent that names the person, unit, and dates. Pair it with an assignment-and-assumption agreement or a carefully written shorter-term handoff document. Fields to cover (outline only — not a form to copy): parties, unit address, start/end of the handoff, rent amount and who pays whom, release decision, deposit plan, and signatures/dates. Legal baseline: §10 written assent.

5. Decide release and the deposit in writing (Practice)

Original tenants are not automatically released after an assignment — see liability and Buck on RentStatute (commercial-facts note included there). For the deposit, pick refund-and-new or transfer-with-acknowledgment and write it down. Do not stretch landlord-side deposit rules onto a tenant handoff; details are on deposit in a handoff and the security deposit page.

6. If someone already moved in without assent

Do not change the locks. After a §10 violation, Oklahoma's §11 path uses a ten-day notice to quit and then court process — not self-help. See §11 and court process and the Oklahoma eviction process page. Ask counsel which notice fits; do not mix §11 clocks with §132 clocks.

7. When to talk to an attorney

  • The lease is longer than two years, or the unit is commercial.
  • You are unsure whether to release the original tenant.
  • Someone already moved in without written assent.
  • The incoming person raises a fair-housing or disability-accommodation issue.
  • The property is subsidized, tax-credit, or takes housing vouchers.

FAQ

Does Oklahoma require written assent before a tenant hands off a lease?

For many short-term and at-will tenancies, yes — 41 O.S. §10 requires written assent to assign or transfer the interest or any part thereof. See the RentStatute lease assignment vs sublease page.

Should I approve an assignment or sign a new lease?

Practice: if the tenant is leaving for good and terms are changing, a new lease is often cleaner. Temporary shorter handoffs need careful written documentation. Legal definitions and liability cites are on RentStatute.
