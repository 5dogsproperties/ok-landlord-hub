Turnover cleaning checklist for lake short-term rentals

A room-by-room checklist for the clean between guests, plus the lake items national checklists miss: docks, kayaks, life jackets, mud and sand.

Informational only — not legal, tax, or insurance advice. These are practice tips from running rentals, not legal requirements. John has owned 300+ units since 2014.

The printable checklist (room by room)

Print this, or copy it into your cleaner's app. Order: trash and linens first, kitchen and baths, floors last, final walk.

Kitchen

  • Run and empty dishwasher; put away clean dishes
  • Wipe counters, stovetop, microwave inside, fridge shelves
  • Toss guest food left in fridge and freezer
  • Empty trash and recycling; new bags in
  • Restock coffee, filters, dish soap, sponges, paper towels

Bedrooms

  • Strip all beds, including pull-outs and bunks
  • Check mattress protectors for stains; swap if needed
  • Make beds with fresh linens to your standard photo
  • Check under beds and in drawers for left items
  • Dust nightstands, lamps, ceiling fan blades

Bathrooms

  • Clean toilet, tub/shower, sink, mirror
  • Replace towels and bath mats
  • Restock toilet paper, soap, shampoo
  • Check drains for hair and slow flow
  • Wipe fixtures; mop floor last

Living areas & exits

  • Vacuum and mop all floors
  • Wipe remotes, light switches, door handles
  • Reset furniture to the listing photos
  • Press the test button on each smoke and CO alarm (per the manufacturer's instructions); replace any that don't sound and tell the host
  • Lock windows and doors; set thermostat to your vacant setting

Lake-specific items

This is where national checklists fall short. Lake guests bring the lake inside.

Dock, deck & watercraft gear

  • Rinse and hang kayaks, paddles, floats
  • Count life jackets against your inventory; set aside any torn or faded ones and tell the host
  • Check dock boards, cleats and ladder for anything loose or broken; photo it
  • Clear fishing line, hooks and bait containers

Mud, sand & outdoor shower

  • Sweep entry mats and shake out rugs
  • Run the outdoor shower; check for drips and clear the drain
  • Spot-clean couch and bedding for lake water or sunscreen stains
  • Check the laundry for wet towels left behind

Grounds & parking

  • Trash cans at the curb or in a closed, animal-proof bin per pickup schedule
  • Pick up cigarette butts and bottle caps by the fire pit
  • Check that boat-trailer parking area is clear
  • Note anything left on the beach or shoreline

Restock list

Consumables

Practice: Set your own par level per item (how many should be in the unit after every clean), write it on the sheet, and have the cleaner flag anything below par.

  • Toilet paper, paper towels, tissues
  • Hand soap, dish soap, dishwasher pods, laundry pods
  • Trash bags (kitchen and outdoor)
  • Coffee, filters, sugar, salt and pepper
  • Sunscreen and bug spray, if you supply them

Linens

Practice: Keep enough linen sets that a set can be in the wash while the next set is on the beds. Your count depends on bed count and turnover pace.

  • Sheet sets per bed
  • Bath towels, hand towels, washcloths
  • Beach or lake towels kept separate from bath towels
  • Spare pillows and blankets

Damage and lost-and-found

Damage

Practice: Platforms set their own windows and rules for damage claims. Check your platform's current host policy before you rely on photos for a claim.

  • Photograph before cleaning anything, with the timestamp on
  • Wide shot, then close-up, of each issue
  • Send photos to the host the same day, before the next guest arrives
  • Note the guest name and checkout date on the report

Lost and found

  • Bag and label each item with the date and the room
  • Photograph it and send to the host
  • Keep a single bin in a locked owner closet
  • Never ship anything without the host's OK

Scheduling same-day turnovers

Summer weekends at the lake stack checkouts and check-ins on the same day. How much time you need between guests depends on the house and the crew; time a few cleans before you set it.

Practice

  • Sync the cleaning calendar to the booking calendar so new bookings auto-create jobs
  • Agree on a checkout time and a check-in time you actually enforce
  • Have a backup cleaner for peak summer weekends
  • Ask the cleaner to send a 'done' photo set, not just a text

Hiring and paying a cleaner

Questions to ask

  • Do you have your own liability insurance? Can you send a certificate?
  • Do you bring supplies or use ours?
  • Who covers if you're sick on a Saturday?
  • Will you send photos at the end of every clean?
  • Can you do laundry on-site or do you take it?

Check this

  • How you pay and classify cleaners (contractor vs employee) is a question for your accountant
  • Whether your insurance covers someone working at the property is a question for your insurance agent
  • Get a W-9 before the first payment

FAQ

What goes on an Airbnb turnover cleaning checklist?

Trash and linens first, then kitchen and bathrooms, then floors, a restock to your par levels, damage and lost-and-found photos, and a final walk against the listing photos. Lake houses add life jackets, watercraft gear, dock checks and mud and sand cleanup.

How much time should I block between guests?

It depends on the size of the house and the crew. Time a few real cleans, add a buffer for laundry and surprises, and set your checkout and check-in times from that.

Should my cleaner report damage, and how?

Yes. Ask for timestamped photos before anything is cleaned, sent to you the same day, with the guest name and checkout date. Check your platform's current damage-claim rules.

Do I need to supply life jackets?

This page doesn't answer that. If you provide life jackets or watercraft, check current Oklahoma and U.S. Army Corps of Engineers lake rules and talk to your insurance agent. If you do supply them, count and inspect them at every turnover.
